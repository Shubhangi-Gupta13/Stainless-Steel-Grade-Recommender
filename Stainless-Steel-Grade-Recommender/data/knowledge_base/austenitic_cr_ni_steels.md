# Austenitic Cr-Ni (300-Series & Heat Resistant) Stainless Steels - Technical Reference

## Microstructure & Fundamental Characteristics
Austenitic Chromium-Nickel stainless steels possess a Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) crystalline structure stabilized primarily by Nickel (8% to 28%) and optionally Nitrogen. Key metallurgical attributes include:
1. **Cryogenic Ductility**: Retains high impact toughness without ductile-to-brittle transition (DBTT) down to cryogenic temperatures (-196°C for standard grades, -269°C for nitrogen-strengthened and super-austenitic grades).
2. **Non-Magnetic Nature**: Fully austenitic structure is non-magnetic in the solution-annealed state ($\mu_r \approx 1.005 - 1.02$). Cold work can induce deformation martensite, slightly raising magnetic susceptibility in lower-Ni grades (e.g., 301, 304). High-nickel grades (305, 316LN, 904L) remain non-magnetic even after heavy deformation.
3. **Formability & Work Hardening**: Remarkable uniform elongation (40%–58%) and excellent deep drawability (Limit Drawing Ratio LDR 2.0–2.3, Erichsen cupping 12–14 mm).

## Sensitization and Carbide Precipitation Mechanism
When austenitic stainless steels are heated or slowly cooled through the critical temperature zone of **450°C to 850°C** (sensitization range, as in welding of heavy sections):
- Carbon diffuses rapidly to grain boundaries and reacts with chromium to precipitate Chromium Carbides ($Cr_{23}C_6$).
- This depletes chromium adjacent to the grain boundaries below the 12% threshold required for passivation.
- The depleted boundary suffers **Intergranular Corrosion (IGC)** or "weld decay" in acidic or chloride environments.

### Metallurgical Solutions:
1. **Low-Carbon "L" Variants ($C \le 0.030\%$)**:
   - Grades: **J-304L, J-316L, J-317L, J-904L**.
   - Carbon is too low to form continuous carbide films during typical welding thermal cycles; no post-weld annealing required.
2. **Titanium / Niobium Stabilization**:
   - Grades: **J-321** (Titanium: $Ti \ge 5 \times (C+N)$) and **J-347** (Niobium: $Nb \ge 10 \times C$).
   - Ti and Nb have a far higher chemical affinity for carbon than chromium, precipitating harmless TiC or NbC and preserving chromium in solid solution. Ideal for continuous service in 450°C–900°C.

## Molybdenum Alloying & Acid/Chloride Resistance
- **J-304 / 304***: Baseline general corrosion resistance. Pitting potential in 3.5% NaCl: 403 mV vs SCE. PREN 20–21.
- **J-316 / J-316L**: 2.0%–3.0% Molybdenum addition stabilizes the passive film against localized breakdown by halide ions ($Cl^-$). PREN 23.5–25.5, CPT 20°C.
- **J-317L / J-317LMN**: 3.0%–5.0% Mo + Nitrogen. PREN 29–34. Designed for flue gas desulfurization (FGD), paper bleach plants, and harsh halides.
- **J-904L (UNS N08904)**: 25% Ni, 4.5% Mo, 1.5% Cu. PREN 34.2, CPT 65°C, CCT 10°C. 25% Ni confers immunity to chloride stress corrosion cracking, while copper provides resistance to reducing sulphuric and hot phosphoric acids.

## Heat-Resistant Austenitic Stainless Steels
- **EN 1.4828 (Cr-Ni-Si)**: 19% Cr, 12% Ni, 2% Si. Destructive scaling temperature >1000°C. Silicon promotes dense protective silica ($SiO_2$) subscale.
- **EN 1.4841 (AISI 314)**: 25% Cr, 20% Ni, 2% Si. Continuous service up to 1200°C in air and carburizing atmospheres.
- **J-309S**: 23% Cr, 13% Ni. Scaling temperature 1090°C.
- **J-310S**: 25% Cr, 20% Ni. Scaling temperature 1150°C. Resists hot alkaline stress cracking.
- **UNS S30815 (EN 1.4835 / 253 MA)**: Micro-alloyed with Rare Earth Metal (Cerium: 0.03%–0.08%) + Nitrogen + Silicon. Creep rupture strength above 850°C exceeds 310S. Maximum service temp 1100°C.
