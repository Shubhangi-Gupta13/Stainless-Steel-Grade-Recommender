# Ferritic Stainless Steels - Technical Reference & Engineering Datasheets

## Microstructure & Metallurgy
Ferritic stainless steels possess a Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) crystal structure identical to alpha-iron ($\alpha$-Fe) at room temperature. They contain 10.5% to 21% Chromium without nickel additions (or minor Ni $\le 1\%$).
Key metallurgical properties include:
1. **Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC) Immunity**: Because the BCC crystal lattice has different slip systems and no susceptible austenitic planar slip bands, ferritic steels are virtually immune to transgranular chloride SCC.
2. **Thermal Conductivity & Expansion**: Compared to austenitic grades, ferritic steels have **50% higher thermal conductivity** (25–27 W/m·K vs 15–16 W/m·K) and **35% lower coefficient of thermal expansion** ($10.0 - 11.5 \times 10^{-6}/\text{K}$ vs $16 - 17.5 \times 10^{-6}/\text{K}$). This makes them resistant to thermal fatigue and scale spalling in cyclic heating.
3. **Ferromagnetic**: Strongly magnetic in all conditions.
4. **Cost Stability**: Being nickel-free, ferritic grades offer immunity to nickel market price spikes.

## Stabilization Metallurgy (Titanium & Niobium)
In standard un-stabilized 430, welding heats the heat-affected zone (HAZ) into the austenite-ferrite two-phase field, leading to martensite formation and grain growth upon cooling.
- **Titanium Stabilization (409L, 439)**:
  $Ti \ge 0.2 + 4 \times (C+N)$ or $6 \times (C+N)$. Titanium scavenges carbon and nitrogen, preventing chromium carbide precipitation and grain boundary sensitization.
- **Dual Stabilization (Nb + Ti in 436L, 441, 444)**:
  - Titanium precipitates at higher temperatures in the molten pool.
  - Niobium precipitates at intermediate temperatures, refining the ferrite grain size and providing high-temperature creep strength.
  - Eliminates post-weld heat treatment requirements and maintains ductility in the as-welded state.

## Grade Performance Highlights
- **EN 1.4003 (UNS S40977 / 3CR12 Utility Ferritic)**:
  - Micro-alloyed structural ferritic steel (11%–12.5% Cr, 0.5% Ni).
  - Atmospheric corrosion rate 0.0015–0.003 mm/year (250× the life of unpainted mild steel).
  - Sliding wear test: **4.7× more wear-resistant than normal mild steel**, 1.6× better than HSLA steel.
  - Weldable up to 30 mm thickness using 309L/316L filler wire.
- **J-409L / J-409Ni**:
  - Benchmark for automotive exhaust manifolds, front pipes, and catalytic shells up to 800°C.
  - J-409Ni includes 0.75% Ni for improved toughness in rail car hoppers and bulk wet materials handling.
- **J-430 / J-430SM / J-430L**:
  - 16%–18% Cr. Excellent polishability, widely used in dishwashers, washing machines, and indoor architectural panels.
  - Resistant to food acids and nitric acid.
- **J-436L & J-441**:
  - J-436L contains 1% Mo + Nb/Ti. Critical pitting potential reaches 499 mV (surpassing 304's 403 mV).
  - J-441: Dual stabilized (18% Cr, Ti+Nb). Resists cyclic oxidation up to 980°C. Standard for exhaust manifolds and solar hot water tanks.
- **J-444 (UNS S44400 / EN 1.4521)**:
  - 18% Cr, 2% Mo, dual stabilized. PREN $\approx 24.5$.
  - Chloride SCC boiling tests: **Resisted 500+ hours in boiling 42% $MgCl_2$ and 26% $NaCl$ with ZERO cracking** (where 304 and 316 fail within hours).
  - Ideal replacement for 316L in hot-water tanks, brewery equipment, and heat exchangers.
- **J-445 (UNS S44500)**:
  - 20% Cr, 0.5% Cu, Nb stabilized. PREN 19.5, pitting potential 330 mV. Corrosion resistance equal to 304 in 500-hour salt spray (ASTM B117) with zero rust.

## Limitations
- Lower cryogenic toughness than austenitics; undergoes ductile-to-brittle transition (DBTT) below 0°C to -20°C in heavy sections.
- Cannot be strengthened by heat treatment (quench hardening).
- Upper continuous service temperature in stressed components limited to 450°C–500°C due to 475°C embrittlement, though unstressed exhaust components function up to 800°C–980°C.
