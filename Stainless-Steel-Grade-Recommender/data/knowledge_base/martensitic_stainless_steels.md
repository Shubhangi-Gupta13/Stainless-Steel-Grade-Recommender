# Martensitic Stainless Steels - Technical Reference & Engineering Datasheets

## Microstructure & Heat Treatment Mechanism
Martensitic stainless steels are straight chromium alloys (11.5% to 18% Cr) containing moderate to high carbon (0.08% to 0.55% C), optionally alloyed with Nickel, Molybdenum, and Vanadium.
- **Phase Transformation**: Unlike austenitic and ferritic steels, martensitic alloys undergo allotropic phase transformation upon heating into the austenite range ($\gamma$-phase, ~950°C–1050°C).
- **Quench Hardening**: Rapid cooling (oil/air quench) traps carbon in a body-centered tetragonal (BCT) supersaturated solid solution (**martensite**).
- **Tempering**: Subsequent tempering between 200°C and 650°C balances hardness, yield strength, and impact toughness.
- **Ferromagnetic**: Strongly magnetic in all heat-treated conditions.

## Grade Performance & Applications
- **J-410 (UNS S41000 / EN 1.4006)**:
  - 12% Cr, 0.12% C.
  - Basic hardenable grade for cutlery, pump shafts, valves, and fasteners. Hardens up to ~40–45 HRC.
  - Continuous service limit: 650°C.
- **J-410DB (Disc Brake Special Grade)**:
  - Specially developed by Jindal Stainless for two-wheeler and four-wheeler disc brakes.
  - High abrasion resistance combined with moderate road-salt corrosion resistance.
  - Hardness 75–88 HRB in supplied condition, optimized for laser cutting and rotor flatness.
- **J-415 (UNS S41500 / EN 1.4313 / CA6NM Type)**:
  - 13% Cr, 4% Ni, 0.7% Mo, low carbon ($C \le 0.05\%$).
  - High yield strength ($\ge 620$ MPa), tensile strength ($\ge 795$ MPa), and Brinell hardness $\le 302$ BHN.
  - Addition of 4% Ni imparts exceptional low-temperature toughness and resistance to cavitation erosion.
  - Standard for hydroelectric turbine runners, Pelton wheels, pump impellers, and oilfield choke valves.
- **J-420J1 & J-420J2 (JIS G4305 / AISI 420)**:
  - 0.20% to 0.35% C. Quench and temper yields hardness of **50 to 54 HRC**.
  - Outstanding cutting edge retention for chef cutlery, dive knives, surgical scalpels, and pump bushings.
- **EN 1.4116 (X50CrMoV15)**:
  - 0.50% C, 14.5% Cr, 0.65% Mo, 0.15% V.
  - World standard for professional German cutlery and precision surgical instruments.
  - Molybdenum enhances pitting resistance, and Vanadium forms hard fine VC carbides for superior razor sharpness.
- **J-431 (UNS S43100 / EN 1.4057)**:
  - 16% Cr, 2% Ni, 0.15% C.
  - Highest corrosion resistance among conventional hardenable martensitic stainless steels.
  - Yield strength $\ge 600$ MPa, tensile strength $\ge 850$ MPa. Heavy machinery shafts, marine propeller rods, aircraft fittings.

## Welding & Fabrication Cautions
> [!CAUTION]
> Martensitic steels have high hardenability. As-welded heat-affected zones (HAZ) form brittle untempered martensite, making them highly prone to hydrogen-induced cold cracking.
> - **Preheating**: Always preheat to 200°C–300°C.
> - **Post-Weld Heat Treatment (PWHT)**: Perform immediate post-weld tempering at 600°C–650°C before the weldment cools to room temperature.
> - High-carbon grades (420J2, 1.4116) should generally **not** be used in welded structural applications.
